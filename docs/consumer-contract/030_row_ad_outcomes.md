## What made the blast radius one read

Not luck, and worth writing down because it is the reason this was a small change
rather than a dangerous one. Every query in `src/domain` that reaches an
`EvidenceUnit` gets there by one of two routes:

- through `Evidence -SUPPORTS|CHALLENGES-> Claim` — `whySupported()`,
  `findingsBearing()`, `decidedOnTheStrengthOf()`, `enquiriesClaiming()`. An
  observation's evidence has **neither** edge; it bears on no claim, which is
  S-11's premise expressed structurally.
- through a required `MATCH (u)-[:USES]->(comp:Computation)` —
  `findingsBearing()` again, `reproductionOf()`, `whatDependsOn()`'s input route.
  An observation unit has no computation.

`whatIsKnown()`'s `worked` test is the only one that reaches a unit by
`ADDRESSES` alone, which is exactly why it was the only read that was wrong and
the only read that changed.

The decision not to mint a `Computation` is therefore load-bearing twice over: it
is honest — LabKit did not run the instrument — *and* it is what kept a
one-invariant fix from silently altering the reproduction verdict and the support
count.

## The asymmetry with `recorded()`, stated because it looks like an oversight

`recorded()` writes `PRODUCES` from the unit to both the evidence **and** the
output artefact. `recordObservations()` writes it only to the evidence.

That is deliberate. In an analysis the artefact is an *output* the unit brought
into existence. In observations the artefact **is** the record of the
measurement, and the unit did not produce it — the unit is the activity of taking
it. Wiring the second edge would claim the record was generated by the act that
describes it. Named in the code, because the next reader will otherwise see an
inconsistency and fix it.

## Atomicity is the half that was not demonstrated

S-9b demonstrated a wrong *read*. Nothing demonstrated a torn write, and the
transaction is not there because a test failed — it is there because **the fix
creates the hazard.**

Before the unit existed, an interrupted `recordObservations()` left a
half-written record: untidy, and nothing the model called impossible. After it, a
failure between the evidence and the unit writes exactly the invariant the change
removes — durably, and **indistinguishable from the eighteen scenarios of records
that predate the fix.** A defect that disguises itself as history is the worst
case available, and it would have been introduced by the change that closed the
row.

Recorded as an argument rather than a demonstration, in the ledger's own
vocabulary (`resolved (argued)`, PJ-017) — except that here the argument
produced a negative test, which was then deletion-verified: with `inTransaction`
removed the test fails, and `write.ts` was restored byte-identical.

## `EvidenceUnitRole` has one writer and no readers

Found while choosing a value for the observation unit. The union has **nine

Adding `"observation"` was the obvious move and was declined. Vocabulary with no

**Left as a finding, not a task.** What would settle it is a reader — the

appear. Until then it is a nine-value union recording one bit.

## Two findings the review added, and they are the same shape at different levels

Both are **declared structure with a writer and no reader**, and the no-cull
policy answers them differently. The contrast is the point.

**`PRODUCES: ["EvidenceUnit", "Artefact"]` is unwalked.** `recorded()` writes it;
nothing reads it. Every `PRODUCES` traversal in `src/` ends at an `Evidence`
except one, and that one starts at a `Computation`. So CLAUDE.md's claim that
every `EDGE_SCHEMA` label has a writer and a reader is true at **label**
granularity and false at **endpoint-pair** granularity — and a label's entry is a
list of pairs, each a separate claim about the domain.

This also answers the question `029` actually asked. The question was *should
`recordObservations()` write the artefact edge too, as `recorded()` does?*
Reframed properly — **name the read that goes wrong** — the answer is that no
read goes wrong either way, because nothing walks the pair. That is a weaker
vindication of the asymmetry than it looks: the reasoning behind it ("the
artefact **is** the record of the measurement, not an output of it") may be right
or wrong and no query would notice. Naming it in the code was still correct, as
an unexplained asymmetry reads as an oversight and invites a symmetry fix.

Kept, not culled, and **named as unwalked** — the policy exists to make untested
claims a computable map, and an unnamed one is a map nobody has.

**`EvidenceUnitRole` gets the opposite answer.** Nine values, one writer, no
readers, and a tenth was declined. The policy protects labels and edges because
they are claims about the domain; a property value is not one, which is the line
the per-tenant CQRS views were removed on.
