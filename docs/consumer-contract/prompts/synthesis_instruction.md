Three designers independently produced a read-only interface design for the same
system, working from the same source material and without contact with one
another. Their complete outputs follow, labelled Designer 1, 2 and 3. Which tool
or person produced each is withheld and is not relevant.

Your task is to produce a **semantic concept map** across the three designs.

Work from meaning, not vocabulary. Two designers using different words for the
same underlying requirement belong in one cluster; one word used by two
designers for different underlying requirements belongs in two. This distinction
is the entire point of the exercise — a map organised by shared vocabulary would
be worthless.

Produce:

## 1. Clusters

For each distinct semantic requirement appearing in one or more designs:

- **A neutral name** you choose — do not privilege any designer's term.
- **What it is**, in two sentences.
- **Which designers require it** (1, 2, 3), and **the exact term each used**.
  Where a designer required it implicitly — an operation cannot work without it,
  but no glossary entry names it — say so explicitly and cite the operation.
- **The distinguishing situations** each designer gave: the two cases that
  become indistinguishable without this concept. Quote them. If designers gave
  materially different distinguishing situations for what you have judged to be
  one cluster, that is important — say so, and consider whether it is really two
  clusters.
- **Whether the concept is a thing that must be remembered, or something
  derivable from other remembered things** — and note any disagreement between
  designers on that point.

Order clusters by how many designers require them, descending. Within a tier,
put the ones with the sharpest distinguishing situations first.

## 2. Singletons

Requirements raised by exactly one designer. Same format. Do not treat these as
weaker or as noise — say plainly what each would buy and what its absence costs,
on the evidence of that designer's own reasoning.

## 3. Genuine disagreements

Places where two or more designers made **incompatible** choices — not different
words, but claims that cannot both be right about how the system should behave.
State each side and what turns on it.

## 4. What none of them asked for

Reading the source requirements quoted in their designs, note any obligation
that appears in the source material but that no design covers.

Rules:

- Do not evaluate the designs, rank the designers, or say which is best.
- Do not propose a data model, schema, or storage design.
- Do not speculate about what system this is for or what will be done with your
  map.
- Quote rather than paraphrase whenever a designer's exact wording carries the
  distinction.
- Where you are unsure whether two things are one cluster, say so and give both
  readings rather than picking silently.
